## What it measures

Shareholders' equity is total assets minus total liabilities, the
company's net worth on its own books. It's the accounting-defined
version of "what's actually left over" once every obligation is
subtracted from everything the company holds.

It grows when a company earns and retains profit, and shrinks when it
takes losses or pays out more than it earns. A steadily growing
shareholders' equity over several years is a reasonable sign a company
is actually building value, not just generating revenue.

## A worked example

A company holds 2 billion in total assets and owes 1.2 billion across
every liability, loans, payables, everything. Shareholders' equity:
2,000,000,000 − 1,200,000,000 = 800,000,000.

## How Kobo & Cents uses it

Shareholders' equity sits in the equity category alongside book value,
and it's also the denominator behind Return on Equity in the
profitability category, worth reading together with that metric, not
only on its own.
