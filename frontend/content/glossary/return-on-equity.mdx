## What it measures

Return on Equity is net income divided by shareholders' equity,
expressed as a percentage. It answers a different question than net
profit margin does: not "how much of revenue becomes profit," but "how
hard is shareholders' own money working."

Two companies can have identical profit margins and very different
ROE if one uses debt to amplify its equity returns and the other
doesn't, worth reading alongside the balance sheet picture, not in
isolation.

## A worked example

A company with 800,000,000 in shareholders' equity reports net income
of 196,800,000 for the year. ROE: 196,800,000 / 800,000,000 = 24.6%.

## How Kobo & Cents uses it

ROE is one of the three profitability metrics, and it's the single
most commonly cited driver in the explainability panel's reasoning,
"ROE is strong and trending up" is a real, frequent sentence there,
because it's often the clearest single signal of whether a company is
actually compounding value for the people who own it.
