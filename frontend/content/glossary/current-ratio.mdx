## What it measures

Current ratio is current assets divided by current liabilities, cash,
inventory, and anything else that converts to cash within a year,
against bills, short-term debt, and other obligations due within that
same year.

A ratio of 1.5 means a company has one and a half naira (or dollars)
of short-term assets for every one it owes in the short term. A ratio
below 1 means its near-term obligations outweigh what it can readily
cover them with, worth understanding why, not automatically a red
flag, but worth understanding why.

## A worked example

A company reports current assets of 450 million and current
liabilities of 300 million. Current ratio: 450 / 300 = 1.5. Read
against sector peers, since a comfortable ratio for a bank looks
nothing like a comfortable ratio for a capital-heavy manufacturer.

## How Kobo & Cents uses it

Current ratio sits in the liquidity category alongside quick ratio.
Both get converted to a percentile against every other stock in the
same sector and market, never judged against a fixed number in
isolation, a P/E-style fixed threshold would mean the same ratio reads
as strong for one sector and weak for another for no real reason.
