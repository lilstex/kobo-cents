## What it measures

Return on Invested Capital is net operating profit after tax, divided
by total invested capital, debt plus equity combined. Where ROE only
looks at shareholders' own money, ROIC looks at every naira or dollar
the business is actually working with, borrowed or owned.

That makes it a fairer efficiency comparison between two companies
with very different capital structures, one funded mostly by
shareholders, one funded mostly by debt, where ROE alone could tell a
misleading story.

## A worked example

A company generates 300,000,000 in after-tax operating profit while
carrying 1.5 billion in total invested capital, debt and equity
combined. ROIC: 300,000,000 / 1,500,000,000 = 20%.

## How Kobo & Cents uses it

ROIC rounds out the profitability category alongside net profit
margin and ROE. Where the three disagree with each other, a company
strong on margin but weak on ROIC, for instance, is exactly the kind
of nuance the explainability panel is built to actually surface, not
flatten into one number.
