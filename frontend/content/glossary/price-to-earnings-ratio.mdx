## What it measures

P/E ratio is share price divided by earnings per share. A P/E of 15
means investors are currently paying 15 times the company's per-share
annual earnings for one share.

A high P/E isn't automatically expensive and a low one isn't
automatically cheap, it depends heavily on what sector a company is
in and how fast it's actually growing. A fast-growing technology
company and a stable, mature bank can both be fairly priced at very
different P/E levels for genuinely different reasons.

## A worked example

A share trades at 91.40 and the company reported earnings of 6.10 per
share over the last year. P/E: 91.40 / 6.10 &asymp; 15.0.

## How Kobo & Cents uses it

P/E sits in the valuation and growth category alongside free cash
flow and revenue growth rate, and like every other metric here, it's
read as a sector-relative percentile, never a fixed "above this number
is expensive" rule that would mean something different for a bank
than it does for a tech company.
