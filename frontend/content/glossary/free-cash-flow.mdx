## What it measures

Free cash flow is cash from operations minus capital expenditure, the
money a business generated from actually running, minus what it spent
maintaining and growing its own equipment, property, and other
long-term assets.

It matters because profit on paper (net income) can differ
meaningfully from actual cash in hand, a company can report a profit
while still spending heavily on new equipment, leaving little real
cash left over, or the reverse. FCF is the harder number to dress up.

## A worked example

A company generates 900,000,000 in cash from operations and spends
250,000,000 on capital expenditure for the year. Free cash flow:
900,000,000 − 250,000,000 = 650,000,000.

## How Kobo & Cents uses it

Free cash flow sits in the valuation and growth category. A company
that's profitable on paper but consistently weak on free cash flow is
exactly the kind of gap the explainability panel is built to call out
directly, rather than leaving a user to notice it themselves by
comparing two separate numbers.
