## What it measures

Dividend yield is annual dividend per share divided by current share
price, expressed as a percentage. It tells you what cash return a
share is currently paying out, separate from whatever the share price
itself does.

Not every company pays a dividend, and that's a deliberate choice, not
a weakness, a fast-growing company reinvesting everything into itself
is making a different, equally valid decision. A missing dividend
yield gets treated that way here too, excluded from scoring rather
than counted as a failure.

## A worked example

A share trades at 91.40 and the company paid 3.75 per share in
dividends over the last year. Dividend yield: 3.75 / 91.40 &asymp;
4.1%.

## How Kobo & Cents uses it

Dividend yield is the dividends category on its own. Payment history,
whether a company has paid consistently over time, sits alongside it
on the stock detail page as supporting context, not folded into this
single number.
